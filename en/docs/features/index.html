Get started

Features

Chats, calls, media, disappearing messages, folders and privacy.

Sinhron aims to feel like a premium messenger while keeping everything end-to-end encrypted. This page walks through what you can do today. Where something is still in beta or not built yet, we say so.

Chats and groups

You can have one-on-one chats and group chats. Every chat, including a one-on-one chat, is an encrypted MLS group under the hood, so the same protection applies everywhere.

To start a chat, open New chat and scan your contact's QR code or paste their key. There are no phone numbers and no public directory, so people can only reach you if you share your code or key with them.

In groups, everyone shares their own display name with the chat, inside the encryption; the server never learns it. The name appears above that person's messages. Until someone has shared a name, a short form of their key is shown. A name is only a claim, so two people can pick the same one: the safety number is what tells them apart.

You can also set a profile photo: in Settings, tap your profile at the top. It travels the same way as your name, inside the encryption, and only to chats you're actually talking in, never to message requests or people you've blocked. Your phone re-encodes the photo first, removing location and camera details, and the other phone re-encodes it again before showing it. It appears in the chat list, the chat header and Chat info.

In a group, type @ to mention someone: you pick from the group's real members. Mentions are stored by the person's key, so they keep pointing at the right person even if names change. When someone mentions you, the chat shows an @ badge until you read it, and the notification comes through even if the chat is muted.

Tap the name at the top of a chat to open Chat info. There you see the members of a group, and you can set disappearing messages, mute the chat, open its media, check safety numbers, block someone, or delete the chat.

The chat list is built for everyday use:

  • Pin important chats to the top. Drag them into the order you want.
  • Mute chats you want to keep quiet.
  • Mark as read or unread, with an unread dot and counters.
  • Drafts are saved per chat and shown in red in the list.
  • Peek at a chat by pressing and holding it: you see the latest messages over a blurred background, with quick actions underneath.
  • A typing indicator shows in the chat header and in the list.

Messages

Press and hold any message to open a menu inspired by iMessage. The message lifts above the blurred chat, with a row of reactions on top and actions below: Reply, Copy, Forward, Pin, Edit, Select and Delete.

  • Reply. Swipe a message to the side, or choose Reply. The quote appears in your message; tap it to jump back to the original.
  • Reactions. Double-tap a text message to send a heart, or pick any reaction from the menu. Tap a reaction chip to add or remove yours.
  • Edit. Fix a typo in your own message within 24 hours of sending it. Edited messages are labelled "edited".
  • Delete. Delete a message just for you, or for everyone (your own messages, within 24 hours). A deleted message really is erased: its text, any media keys, the quote and reactions. After 24 hours, nobody's app accepts an edit or delete of the message, so old history can't be rewritten.
  • Pin. Pin a message to a bar under the chat header, for everyone in the chat. Tap the bar to jump to the message.
  • Select and forward. Select several messages to copy, delete or forward them. Forwarded photos and files are re-encrypted under a new key, so the server cannot link the two chats.
  • Status ticks. Sending, sent, delivered and read.

Formatting works with simple markers: *bold*, _italic_, `monospace`, ~strikethrough~, and ||spoiler|| (hidden until tapped). Links and phone numbers are detected, and a message with one to three emoji is shown large.

Search looks through chat names and message text, in any letter case. It uses a full-text index kept inside your encrypted local database, so it stays fast in long histories. Tap a result and the chat opens at that message. Search runs entirely on your device; the server never sees what you search for.

Photos, video, files and voice

In beta

Media features are implemented and covered by tests, but have not yet been verified end to end on a real device. Expect rough edges.

Every attachment is encrypted on your phone with a fresh key before it is uploaded. The server stores only scrambled bytes; the key travels inside your encrypted message.

  • Photos. Send from the camera or your library, up to 10 at once, with a caption. Several photos arrive together as an album. Before sending, each photo is re-encoded on your device, which strips location (GPS) and other hidden metadata.
  • Gallery. Tap a photo to open it full screen. Swipe through every photo in the chat, pinch or double-tap to zoom, swipe down to close. You can share a photo or save it to your library.
  • Video. Record a video with the camera (up to 3 minutes) or send one from your library. Large clips are compressed to 720p on your phone before they're encrypted, and a still frame travels with the video (encrypted, like a photo thumbnail) so the chat shows a preview. Playback happens from memory, so the decrypted video is never written to disk.
  • Files. Send documents of any type. Open or share them with other apps; the temporary copy is deleted as soon as you are done.
  • Voice messages. Hold the microphone to record, slide left to cancel, or slide up to lock and keep recording hands-free. Play back at 1×, 1.5× or 2×; consecutive voice messages play one after another.
  • Link previews. Off by default. If you turn them on, the preview is built by your phone when you send; the people you send it to never contact the website. See Security.

The chat's Media screen collects all photos, files, voice messages and links in one place.

Calls

Sinhron supports audio and video calls. Calls are end-to-end encrypted with SFrame (RFC 9605), using keys derived from your chat's MLS encryption. The call server forwards the media but cannot see or hear it. Calls can get through restrictive networks that only allow ordinary HTTPS traffic.

If you'd rather not be called, turn off Incoming calls in Privacy: every call is then declined without ringing.

Current limitations:

  • Group calls are in beta. They are built, but have not yet been tested on real devices.
  • Incoming calls only ring while the app is open. Ringing on a locked or closed phone requires Apple's push service, which needs an Apple developer certificate that is not set up yet.
  • Calls across different networks on a real deployed server have not been fully tested yet.

Disappearing messages

You can make messages in a chat disappear after a set time. Open Chat info (tap the chat's name at the top) and choose Off, 5 minutes, 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week or 4 weeks.

Both people see a note when the timer changes, the header shows the current timer, and disappearing messages carry a small timer icon. When the time is up, the message is deleted from both phones completely: the text, photos, video, voice messages and documents, along with their encryption keys.

A few things to know:

  • The timer starts when a message is sent (for you) or when it arrives on the other phone, not when it is read.
  • The server can't read disappearing messages, but it does know a chat's timer: it uses it to delete that chat's stored encrypted messages early.
  • Disappearing messages protect against leaks later on. They cannot stop the other person from taking a screenshot. On iPhone, if someone takes a screenshot while a chat with disappearing messages is open, everyone in the chat sees a note about it. The same goes for recording or mirroring the screen: the chat notes it once per recording. A modified app or a second phone's camera won't trigger it, so treat it as a courtesy, not protection.

Folders and archive

Organise your chats into folders. Built-in folders are All, Personal, Groups and Unread. You can also create your own: include all personal chats, all groups, or specific chats, and hide muted or read chats.

  • Swipe left and right to move between folders. Each folder tab shows an unread count.
  • Press and hold a folder tab to edit, reorder (drag them), mark all as read, or delete it.
  • Archive chats you want out of the way. Archived chats live in their own screen and do not appear in any folder. In a long list, the Archive row waits above your first chat: pull down to reveal it.
  • A new message brings an archived chat back to the list, unless the chat is muted. Muted chats stay archived.
  • Swipe actions on a chat row: mute or unmute, archive or unarchive, and delete (always with a confirmation).

App lock and privacy

  • App lock. Require Face ID, Touch ID or your device passcode to open Sinhron. Choose when it locks: immediately, or after 1, 5 or 60 minutes in the background.
  • Hide in app switcher. When Sinhron is in the background, a blurred cover with the logo hides your chats from the app switcher. On by default.
  • Read receipts and typing indicators can be turned off. Like in Signal, they are mutual: if you turn them off, you neither send nor see them.
  • Online status is never shown.
  • Link previews are off unless you turn them on.
  • Incoming calls can be switched off entirely.
  • Proxy (Tor). Route all traffic to the server through a SOCKS5 proxy such as Orbot, so the server doesn't see your IP address. Calls don't go through the proxy.

Message requests and blocking

When someone you haven't talked to starts a chat with you, it arrives as a message request. Until you accept, they don't see that you've read their messages, don't see you typing, don't learn your name, and their calls don't ring. Instead of the message field you see Accept, Block and Delete. Writing in the chat accepts it too.

Block someone from a chat's info screen, or from their row in a group's member list. Their messages, reactions and other actions are dropped on your phone before you ever see them, and calls from them in your personal chat are declined without ringing. You can review and unblock people under Privacy → Blocked.

Blocking stays on your device, so the server never learns who you've blocked. In a group, a blocked person is still a member: they can still read what you write there, you just don't see them.

All of these live on one Privacy screen, and every switch on it does exactly what it says.

Verifying contacts

To be sure you are talking to the right person, and that nobody is in the middle, compare safety numbers. Each chat has a 60-digit number (12 groups of 5 digits) computed from both of your keys. It is the same on both phones.

Compare it in person or over a channel you trust. Or tap Scan their code and point the camera at the QR code on their screen: if the codes match, the contact is marked as verified right away, and if they don't, you get a warning. You can also tap Mark as verified yourself, and a checkmark appears next to their name. If your contact's key ever changes, Sinhron shows "Safety number changed" and removes the checkmark, so you can verify again.

In groups, every member has their own safety number. Open Chat info and tap a member to check theirs.

Appearance and accessibility

  • Themes: System, Light, Dark and Black (OLED).
  • Six accent colours, adjusted in dark mode for contrast.
  • Chat wallpapers: soft gradients, subtle patterns, or your own photo (kept only on your device).
  • Languages: English and Russian. Sinhron follows your phone's language, or you can choose one in Settings → Appearance → Language.
  • VoiceOver: buttons, tabs, chat rows and messages have spoken labels, and message actions are available from VoiceOver.
  • Larger text: key screens are tested with Dynamic Type at 1.5× and 2× size.
  • Contrast: text and accent colours meet WCAG AA contrast in light and dark themes.
  • Reduce Motion is respected: slides become cross-fades and springy effects are turned off.
  • Sounds and haptics: quiet send and receive sounds and subtle vibrations, each with its own switch.
